Field Note #1: Demo-to-qualified-call handoff QA

If your site has more than one path toward a demo, quote, or sales conversation, the first useful audit is not “install more AI.” It is simpler: make sure a warm visitor can understand what happens next and does not have to restart their context at every step.

Careful language matters: a public page can show friction. It does not prove lost revenue by itself. The right claim is “possible friction,” not “you are losing money.”
1. CTA expectation map
List every public CTA that implies a sales/demo path. Compare the words, destination, and next expectation.
2. Form-to-meeting handoff
Without submitting anything, inspect whether the public path suggests context carries forward or whether the visitor may need to repeat themselves.
3. Routing clarity
Check whether sales, support, security, partnership, and enterprise intent have visibly different paths or all collapse into one vague inbox.

The 10-minute public-only pass

  1. Open the homepage on mobile and desktop.
  2. Write down every button that sounds like Get a Demo, Book a Demo, Contact Sales, Talk to Us, or Start.
  3. Click only public paths. Do not submit forms. Do not enter private information.
  4. For each path, answer: “What happens next?” If the answer changes depending on the button, flag it.
  5. Look for a public email or contact path. If it exists, note whether the right buyer knows when to use it.
  6. Write one sentence describing the possible friction without accusing the company of losing money.

Field Note #2: After the form — expectation & confirmation checklist

Most capture paths stop auditing at “there is a form.” The next dollar-facing check is what a warm visitor can tell about the after: who responds, how soon, what the next step is, and whether they need to prepare anything. Public-only. No submit. No private data.

Why this matters for AI/SaaS and high-intent service paths: vendor-reported B2B demo-to-close benchmarks often land around ~25% overall and ~30% for SaaS (Optifai Sales Ops Benchmark, N=939, Q2 2025–Q1 2026). That is not a promise for any one site. It is a reason to treat pre-demo qualification and post-request expectation clarity as product, not decoration. Separate principle: journey-friction audits repeatedly flag “someone will be in touch” loops as clarity friction — the visitor started a high-intent action and still cannot answer “what happens next?”
1. Pre-form instruction
Before the form, does the page say what the form is for, what is required, and what good answers look like?
2. Response expectation
Can a visitor see who replies, the channel (email/calendar/call), and a realistic timing window?
3. Qualification honesty
If the team only books “good fit” demos, is that said up front so tire-kickers and buyers both know the bar?
4. Confirmation path (public)
Without submitting: is there visible copy about a confirmation page, email, calendar invite, or “we received it” expectation?
5. Preparation ask
Does the path tell the visitor what to bring (use case, stack, team size, urgency) so the first human conversation is sharper?
6. Dead-end risk
After the CTA, does everything collapse into a vague inbox with no owner, timing, or alternate route?

Worksheet: multi-intent routing map

Field Note #1’s third check is routing clarity. Use this when a site has more than one high-intent door — demo, sales, support, security, partners, enterprise, careers — and you need to see whether those intents stay separated in public or collapse into one vague inbox.

Public-only rule: inspect labels, menus, footer links, and destination first screens. Do not submit forms, open tickets, log in, or claim that mixed routing proves lost revenue. The useful claim is “intent may collide,” not “leads are dying.”
1. List the doors
Write every public path that implies a different owner or urgency.
2. Name the owner guess
Sales, CS, security, partnerships, recruiting, billing — based only on public labels.
3. Trace the landing
Same form, same mailto, same chat, or a visibly different destination?
4. Flag collision risk
If two different intents land in the same bucket with no routing note, mark yellow/red.
