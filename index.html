Field Note #1: Demo-to-qualified-call handoff QA

If your site has more than one path toward a demo, quote, or sales conversation, the first useful audit is not “install more AI.” It is simpler: make sure a warm visitor can understand what happens next and does not have to restart their context at every step.

Careful language matters: a public page can show friction. It does not prove lost revenue by itself. The right claim is “possible friction,” not “you are losing money.”
1. CTA expectation map
List every public CTA that implies a sales/demo path. Compare the words, destination, and next expectation.
2. Form-to-meeting handoff
Without submitting anything, inspect whether the public path suggests context carries forward or whether the visitor may need to repeat themselves.
3. Routing clarity
Check whether sales, support, security, partnership, and enterprise intent have visibly different paths or all collapse into one vague inbox.

The 10-minute public-only pass

  1. Open the homepage on mobile and desktop.
  2. Write down every button that sounds like Get a Demo, Book a Demo, Contact Sales, Talk to Us, or Start.
  3. Click only public paths. Do not submit forms. Do not enter private information.
  4. For each path, answer: “What happens next?” If the answer changes depending on the button, flag it.
  5. Look for a public email or contact path. If it exists, note whether the right buyer knows when to use it.
  6. Write one sentence describing the possible friction without accusing the company of losing money.

Template: public-only teardown note

Use this when you want to turn a quick check into a buyer-safe one-page note. It keeps the work useful without pretending a public page proves revenue loss.

Block What to write Safe wording
1. Public path checkedHomepage, pricing page, demo page, contact page, booking path, or public profile.“I checked only public pages and did not submit forms.”
2. Visitor intentWhat the visitor is likely trying to do: book, compare, ask, buy, or route a support/sales question.“A visitor may expect…”
3. Evidence screenshotDescribe the visible UI/text/state. Use screenshots in private work; do not scrape or expose private data.“The public page shows…”
4. Possible frictionName the uncertainty, mismatch, repeat step, unclear routing, or dead-end.“This could add friction because…”
5. Low-risk fix ideaOne plain improvement: clearer CTA text, routing note, confirmation copy, handoff explanation, or checklist.“A simple test would be…”
6. Next questionAsk permission before sending more, requesting data, or proposing implementation.“Would a one-page example be useful?”
Boundary: do not claim broken funnels, lost revenue, compliance issues, security problems, accessibility failures, SEO wins, ad waste, or guaranteed outcomes from a public-only pass.

Example one-page note you could send after permission

Subject: Tiny public-only demo handoff note

I checked only the public demo path and did not submit the form. The CTA and demo page match pretty well. The one possible friction point is the handoff after the embedded form: a buyer can tell they are requesting a demo, but may not know who replies, how soon, or whether the next step is email or calendar.

A simple test would be adding one line near the form: “Tell us what you are trying to automate; a team member will review and reply by email with the best next step.”

If useful, I can turn this into a one-page public-only checklist for the rest of the path. If not, no need to reply.

Safe outreach phrasing

Use this:

“I noticed a possible demo/contact handoff issue that may be creating friction. Would it be okay if I sent a one-page public-only example with the checks I’d run?”

Not this:

“Your site is leaking money and I found critical conversion failures.”
