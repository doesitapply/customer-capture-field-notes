Make the handoff legible before proposing a bigger change.

This printable, fictional example shows how to turn one vague public CTA into a small evidence record and one clearer next-step cue. Use the method on public words and destinations only.

Fictional example; not a teardown of a real business. Do not use this page to infer traffic, conversion, revenue, accessibility, compliance, security, or customer behavior. It names possible friction to check—not an outcome.

Worked example: promise → action → handoff

Imagine a fictional planning-tool homepage. The operator wants a first-time evaluator to understand pricing before creating an account.

public-only fictional one path no submission

1. Bound the path

Visible promise“Plan every launch in one place.”
Primary action“Get started”
Immediate destinationAccount-creation screen

2. Preserve the literal public evidence

Public cueWhat a visitor can see nextPossible friction to check
“Plan every launch in one place.” A general invitation to begin. The promise does not say whether the first click compares plans, opens a product tour, or begins registration.
“Get started” An account-creation screen. A visitor who only wants to compare pricing may not expect account creation as the immediate handoff.

Observation, not diagnosis: the wording may be perfectly intentional. Capture it literally, then ask the owner what one visitor action matters most before changing it.

3. Draft one proportionate clarification

Original public cue

Get started

It can mean several different next steps without describing the first handoff.

Candidate cue, if price comparison is the intended action

Compare plans — see pricing and choose your next step

It names the desired outcome and says what happens immediately after the click.

4. Ask one owner question before a larger change

“For a first-time evaluator, is the intended next step to compare plans, take a product tour, or create an account?”

If the answer is “compare plans,” the candidate cue may be worth internal review. If the answer is “create an account,” use a different cue that says so plainly. Do not jump from this single public observation to a redesign or a performance claim.

Reuse the pattern

  1. Quote the visible promise and action exactly.
  2. Name the first visible handoff after the action.
  3. Write one possible-friction question, not a conclusion.
  4. Draft one cue in the pattern: “[Verb] [specific outcome] — [what happens next].”
  5. Ask the owner whether that is the intended visitor action.

Boundary: this is a public observation and writing aid. It does not collect data, test accounts or checkout, request private information, assess security/accessibility/compliance, or establish business results.
